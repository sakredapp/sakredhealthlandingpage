Healthy cinnamon rolls are made with a tangzhong dough — a cooked flour-and-water paste that lets the bread hold far more moisture — filled with whole-date paste and Ceylon cinnamon instead of a butter-and-brown-sugar slurry, and finished with a Greek yogurt glaze. They come out at roughly **7 grams of added sugar per roll, or about 17 grams of total sugar including the dates**, against 59 grams of added sugar in a Cinnabon Classic Roll.

Both of those numbers are printed here on purpose, because they answer different questions and a lot of recipes only print the flattering one. There is also a separate issue with cinnamon rolls that has nothing to do with sugar: the cinnamon. Most of what is sold in American supermarkets is cassia, and European food safety authorities have a daily limit for one of its compounds that a single teaspoon can reach on its own.

*This article is educational, not medical or nutrition advice. Talk to your doctor before making dietary changes, especially if you're pregnant, nursing, managing blood sugar, or taking medication.*

## The cinnamon problem nobody mentions

Cinnamon is not one plant. "Ceylon cinnamon" is *Cinnamomum verum*, grown mainly in Sri Lanka. Nearly everything else sold as cinnamon — including most supermarket ground cinnamon in the United States — is cassia, from several related *Cinnamomum* species. They taste different, cassia being sharper and more aggressive, and Ceylon more floral. They also differ in a compound called coumarin.

Coumarin is hepatotoxic at high intake. The European Food Safety Authority and Germany's Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) set a tolerable daily intake of **0.1 mg of coumarin per kilogram of body weight** — an amount considered safe every day across a lifetime. BfR's own worked example is the part that stops you: for a 60-kilogram adult, **2 grams of cassia cinnamon with average coumarin content reaches that limit**. For a 15-kilogram child, it takes half a gram.

Two grams is well under a level teaspoon.

This recipe uses about 1.3 grams of cinnamon per roll. Made with average-coumarin cassia, that is roughly 3.9 mg — about 65% of a 60-kilogram adult's daily tolerable intake in one roll. Made with Ceylon, whose measured coumarin content runs an order of magnitude lower, it is around 0.24 mg, or about 4%.

**Two things keep this from being alarmism.** BfR explicitly states that exceeding the TDI somewhat for one to two weeks is not a health concern — this is about habitual intake, not a holiday morning. And the TDI is set with a safety margin below levels where effects were observed, in sensitive individuals, from pharmaceutical coumarin. Nobody is saying a cinnamon roll will hurt your liver.

What the numbers do say is that if you use cinnamon daily — in coffee, oatmeal, smoothies, a spice-heavy baking season — the variety is worth choosing deliberately. Ceylon costs more and is usually sold by name. Cassia is what "cinnamon" means by default.

## And the lead issue, which is separate and current

In October 2023 the FDA began investigating children with elevated blood lead levels, and by early 2024 identified cinnamon in apple purée pouches as the likely source. That investigation widened into a standing public health alert on ground cinnamon products found to contain elevated lead, with the FDA expanding its list to 16 brands as of October 2025. Reported concentrations in state and FDA sampling ranged from roughly 2 to 7.7 parts per million.

This is a supply-chain contamination problem, not a property of the spice, and it is confined to specific products the FDA names. The practical response is not to fear cinnamon; it is to check the FDA's current alert list against what is in your cabinet, and to buy from suppliers who publish testing. That is a two-minute task that most people baking through a holiday season have never been told to do.

## About the sweetener

Both versions use real sugar. That is not the difference, and saying otherwise invites an easy correction — the commercial product is sweetened with cane sugar, not an artificial sweetener.

What differs is **what the sugar arrives inside**. This recipe sweetens with whole Medjool dates. Dates are more than 70% sugar, which we are not going to pretend away — and yet across five varieties tested in both healthy and type 2 diabetic subjects they measured a **glycemic index of roughly 44 to 55**, low-to-medium, with no significant post-meal glucose excursions in the diabetic group ([Alkaabi et al., *Nutrition Journal*, 2011](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3112406/)). The fruit's fiber is still attached and still doing its job. Strain it out into a syrup and that advantage leaves with it.

So the claim is not that this sugar is free. FDA counts dates, maple and coconut sugar as added sugars, and so do we. The claim is **less sugar, and sugar that still has its fiber**. ## Ingredients

- 3 tablespoons bread flour, for the tangzhong
- 1/2 cup water, for the tangzhong
- 3 1/4 cups bread flour
- 2 1/4 teaspoons instant yeast
- 1 teaspoon fine sea salt
- 1/2 cup whole milk, warmed to about 100°F
- 1 large egg plus 1 egg yolk, room temperature
- 3 tablespoons pure maple syrup
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, softened
- 1/2 cup whole-date paste (about 8 Medjool dates, pitted and blended smooth)
- 6 tablespoons unsalted butter, very soft, for the filling
- 2 tablespoons ground Ceylon cinnamon
- 2 tablespoons coconut sugar
- 1/4 teaspoon fine sea salt, for the filling
- 1/2 cup plain whole-milk Greek yogurt
- 2 tablespoons pure maple syrup, for the glaze
- 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract

## Instructions

1. Make the tangzhong: whisk 3 tablespoons bread flour into 1/2 cup water in a small saucepan and cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until it thickens into a smooth paste that holds a line from the whisk — about 2 minutes. Scrape into a bowl and cool to room temperature.
2. In a stand mixer with a dough hook, combine the remaining bread flour, yeast and 1 teaspoon salt. Add the cooled tangzhong, warm milk, egg, egg yolk and 3 tablespoons maple syrup.
3. Mix on low until a shaggy dough forms, then knead on medium-low for 6 minutes.
4. Add the 4 tablespoons softened butter a tablespoon at a time, waiting for each to disappear. Knead another 8 to 10 minutes, until the dough is smooth, glossy and pulls cleanly from the bowl.
5. Cover and let rise in a warm place until doubled, 60 to 90 minutes.
6. Make the filling: blend the pitted dates with 2 to 3 tablespoons hot water until completely smooth, then beat in the 6 tablespoons soft butter, Ceylon cinnamon, coconut sugar and 1/4 teaspoon salt.
7. Roll the dough on a lightly floured surface into a 16 by 12-inch rectangle. Spread the filling edge to edge, leaving a half-inch border along one long side.
8. Roll up tightly from the long side toward the bare border, then cut into 12 equal pieces with unflavored dental floss or a very sharp knife.
9. Arrange in a buttered 9 by 13-inch pan, cover, and let rise until puffy and touching, 45 to 60 minutes.
10. Heat the oven to 350°F and bake 22 to 26 minutes, until the centers register 190°F and the tops are deep gold.
11. Whisk the Greek yogurt, 2 tablespoons maple syrup and vanilla. Spread over the rolls while they are warm but not hot, so the glaze sets rather than melts.
12. Serve warm. Store covered in the refrigerator up to 3 days; the yogurt glaze is perishable.

## What do cinnamon rolls do to blood sugar?

This is now a question people can answer for themselves, which is new. In March 2024 the FDA cleared Dexcom's Stelo as the first over-the-counter continuous glucose monitor, and Abbott's Lingo and Libre Rio followed in June 2024. Lingo is not cleared for use in diabetes at all — it is sold as a general wellness tool. Millions of people without a diagnosis can now watch a pastry move a line on a phone.

So it is worth saying clearly what this recipe does and does not change.

**A cinnamon roll is a high-carbohydrate food and this one still is.** Bread flour, dates and maple are all glucose in the end. Roughly 17 grams of total sugar plus the flour's starch will produce a real postprandial rise. Cutting added sugar from 59 grams to 7 substantially reduces the load; it does not make it a low-carbohydrate food, and no honest recipe should imply otherwise.

**What has better evidence than any ingredient swap is the order you eat things in.** Shukla and colleagues, publishing in *Diabetes Care* in 2015, gave the same meal to overweight adults with type 2 diabetes on two occasions a week apart, changing only the sequence. When vegetables and protein were eaten before the carbohydrate, mean post-meal glucose fell 28.6% at 30 minutes, 36.7% at 60, and 16.8% at 120 — and the incremental area under the curve across two hours was **73% lower**. The group extended the finding to prediabetes in 2019, and a randomized pilot in 2023 tested it as a behavioral intervention.

That is a small study — 11 subjects in the original — and it was done in a clinical setting with a defined meal. But the direction has replicated across populations, including in gestational diabetes, and the mechanism is not exotic: protein and fiber slow gastric emptying and stimulate incretin release before the starch arrives.

The practical version for a holiday morning: eat the eggs first. A cinnamon roll after two scrambled eggs and a handful of greens is a different glucose event from the same roll eaten alone on an empty stomach at 9 a.m. That costs nothing, requires no ingredient substitution, and is better supported than most of what gets written about "blood sugar friendly" baking.

**And to be explicit about what is not claimed here:** there is no reliable evidence that the cinnamon in these rolls controls blood sugar. Clinical trial results are mixed and the effect sizes, where found, are small and inconsistent. Cinnamon is in this recipe for flavor and aroma, which is how it earns its place — warm spice raises perceived sweetness, letting the filling work at a fraction of the usual sugar. ## Why tangzhong instead of more butter and sugar

Because the softness people want from a cinnamon roll is a water problem, not a fat problem, and most recipes solve it with sugar.

Cooking a small portion of the flour with water gelatinizes its starch, which lets that starch hold several times its weight in water. Folding the resulting paste back into the dough raises the total moisture the bread can carry without turning the dough slack and unworkable. The result is a crumb that stays soft for days, because free water that would otherwise evaporate is bound inside gelatinized starch.

Sugar does a version of the same job — it is hygroscopic, holding water and slowing staling — which is precisely why low-sugar baked goods go stale fast and why so many "healthy" cinnamon rolls are dry by lunch. Tangzhong replaces that function structurally rather than with more sweetener. It is the single technique that makes a 7-gram roll worth eating.

The date paste helps too. Dates carry pectin and fiber along with their sugar, so the filling thickens as it bakes rather than running out of the spiral into the pan.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I buy Ceylon cinnamon?

It is stocked in most well-supplied grocery stores, spice shops and online, and it will be labeled "Ceylon" or *Cinnamomum verum* by name — if a jar just says "cinnamon," assume cassia. Ceylon costs noticeably more and is softer and more crumbly in stick form, where cassia sticks are hard, thick and tightly rolled. Given the FDA's current lead alert on specific ground cinnamon products, buying from a supplier that publishes heavy-metal testing is worth the premium regardless of variety.

### Can I make these dairy-free?

Yes. Use a plant butter with at least 75% fat in both the dough and filling, unsweetened oat milk in the dough, and a thick coconut yogurt for the glaze. The crumb will be slightly less tender because milk proteins contribute to softness, so add one extra tablespoon of tangzhong water to compensate.

### Can I prepare them the night before?

Yes, and this is the best way to serve them. Complete through step 9, cover the pan tightly and refrigerate for up to 16 hours. Take the pan out 45 minutes before baking to let the dough finish rising, then bake as directed, adding 3 to 5 minutes. Make the glaze fresh.

### Is 7 grams of added sugar per roll actually low for a cinnamon roll?

For this category, yes — it is roughly a twelfth of a Cinnabon Classic. In absolute terms it is about 28% of the American Heart Association's daily limit for women, and the roll also carries about 10 more grams of sugar from dates plus the flour's starch. If you are tracking glucose, count the whole roll, not the added-sugar line. The point of cooking it yourself is that both numbers are knowable.
