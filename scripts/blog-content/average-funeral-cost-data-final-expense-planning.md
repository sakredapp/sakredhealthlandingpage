The gap between what a funeral costs and what most families have set aside for one is wide enough to measure precisely. According to the National Funeral Directors Association's most recent General Price List survey, the national median cost of a funeral with viewing and burial is $8,300 — $9,995 once a burial vault is included. Meanwhile, the one federal benefit designed to help — Social Security's lump-sum death payment — is $255, a figure that has been capped at that amount since 1954, according to the Congressional Research Service. That mismatch, more than any sales pitch, is why final expense planning exists. Here is what the data actually says.

## The itemized bill: where $9,995 actually goes

The NFDA's General Price List survey — the industry's benchmark pricing study, based on hundreds of member funeral homes — breaks the median funeral into line items. The 2023 edition, the most recent, looks like this:

|---|---|
| Basic services fee (non-declinable) | $2,495 |
| Removal/transfer of remains to funeral home | $395 |
| Embalming | $845 |

| Facilities and staff for viewing | $475 |
| Facilities and staff for funeral ceremony | $550 |

Two things in this table deserve attention. First, the basic services fee — $2,495 — is non-declinable under the FTC's Funeral Rule; it applies no matter how simple the arrangement. Second, and more important for planning: **this total excludes the cemetery entirely.** The NFDA figure does not include the plot, opening and closing of the grave, a monument or marker, flowers, or the obituary. Families budgeting off the $8,300 headline number routinely find the real total runs thousands higher once cemetery charges are added.

Cremation shifts the math but doesn't eliminate it. The NFDA's median for a funeral with cremation — including viewing, ceremony, an alternative cremation casket, and an urn (median urn cost: $295) — is $6,280. A direct cremation with no services costs far less, but most families still hold some form of gathering.

## Cremation is rewriting the industry — not the need to plan

The NFDA's 2025 Cremation & Burial Report, released in September 2025, projects the U.S. cremation rate at 63.4% for 2025, more than double the 31.6% burial rate. The trajectory is steep: by 2035, cremation is projected to exceed 50% in every state and Washington, D.C., and by 2045 it reaches 82.3% nationally, with burial falling to 13%. The NFDA attributes the shift to affordability, looser religious constraints, environmental preferences, and families spread across the country.

For planning purposes, the takeaway is nuanced. Cremation is cheaper than burial, but the $2,000 gap between the two medians ($6,280 versus $8,300) is smaller than most people assume — because the service, staff, facilities, and transportation costs are largely the same either way. Choosing cremation reduces the bill; it does not remove it.

## Where you live changes the number

Funeral pricing is regional. Analyzing the NFDA's 2023 regional data, SmartAsset reports that New England has the highest median cost for a funeral with viewing and burial at $8,985, with the Middle Atlantic states close behind at $8,573. New England is also the most expensive region for cremation with services at $7,023, while the Mountain region is the least expensive at roughly $5,500. That's a spread of about $1,500 or more on an identical service depending on geography — worth knowing if you're pricing coverage while living in one state and expecting services in another.

## The inflation picture: slower lately, relentless over decades

Here the data cuts both ways, and honest planning should account for both.

In the short run, funeral inflation has actually trailed general inflation. The NFDA's 2023 General Price List study found the median burial funeral rose 5.8% between 2021 and 2023 (from $7,848 to $8,300) and cremation rose 8.1% (from $5,810 to $6,280) — while overall CPI rose 13.6% over the same two years.

Over decades, the opposite is true. The Bureau of Labor Statistics found that from December 1986 to September 2017, funeral expenses rose 227.1% while prices for all items rose 123.4% — funerals inflated at nearly twice the pace of everything else. The Official Data Foundation's analysis of the same BLS series puts average funeral-expense inflation at 3.65% per year from 1986 through 2026, versus 2.78% for overall CPI. A policy sized to today's median without any cushion will likely fall short of the median twenty years from now.

## How families actually pay — and where it goes wrong

The affordability data is the strongest argument for planning ahead, and it has deteriorated quickly. In Debt.com's October 2025 survey of 1,000 U.S. adults, 37% said they took on debt after a loved one's death — up from 14% just one year earlier. Among those who borrowed, 59% put funeral costs on credit cards and 38% took out personal loans. Fifty-seven percent said they could not cover a loved one's funeral today without going into debt, and roughly one in four carrying funeral debt reported it caused ongoing anxiety.

Crowdfunding has become the visible symptom of this gap. GoFundMe has reported that more than 125,000 memorial fundraisers are created on its platform each year, raising over $330 million annually — figures cited by North American Company in its analysis of funeral crowdfunding. A memorial fundraiser can help, but it is unpredictable by design: it depends on timing, network size, and public sympathy, none of which a grieving family controls.

And the federal backstop is symbolic. The Social Security Administration's lump-sum death payment is a one-time $255, payable only to a qualifying surviving spouse or child, with a two-year application window. Per the Congressional Research Service, the cap hasn't moved since 1954. Against a $9,995 median funeral, it covers about 2.5%.

## Sizing coverage to the actual numbers

Final expense insurance (also called burial insurance) is a small whole life policy built for exactly this bill. Working from the data above, a reasonable sizing exercise looks like this:

- **Floor:** $6,280 (median cremation with services) — if your family is certain about cremation and your region is average-to-low cost.
- **Median case:** $10,000–$12,000 — covers the $9,995 burial-with-vault median plus a margin for cemetery charges the NFDA figure excludes.
- **With inflation cushion:** $15,000–$25,000 — appropriate if the policy may not pay out for 15–20 years, given the long-run 3.65% annual funeral inflation rate, or if you want it to also clear small debts and end-of-life medical bills.

The market matches this math. Final expense policies are typically issued in amounts from $5,000 to $50,000, and most buyers land in the $10,000–$20,000 range — enough to clear the burial-with-vault median plus cemetery and transition costs. These policies are typically simplified-issue — no medical exam, a few health questions — which is why they remain accessible at ages 50–85 when term coverage gets expensive or unavailable. ## The two-number summary

If you remember nothing else from this data, remember $9,995 and $255: the median cost of a burial funeral with vault, and what the federal government contributes toward it. Everything in between — the 57% of families who'd need debt to close that gap, the 125,000 memorial fundraisers a year, the credit cards carrying grief at 20%-plus interest — is what happens when the difference goes unplanned. A policy sized in an afternoon, using the medians above plus a cushion for your region and your timeline, is the boring, effective alternative.
